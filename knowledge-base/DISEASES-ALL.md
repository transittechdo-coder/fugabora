# FugaBora: Complete Animal Disease Reference (Draft 1)

**Status: research draft. Needs vet review before anything goes into the app.**
Compiled 31 Oct 2026 from material the team gathered (Google AI search summaries of Ministry of Livestock and Fisheries, TVLA and VCT documents) plus FugaBora's own Stage 1 list.

> ⚠️ AI search summaries can contain mistakes. Every entry must be checked against the official documents listed in **Section 9** and signed off by a vet.
> ⚠️ **Drug names and doses (Section 8) are for the team's reference only. They must NEVER be shown in the app.** The app only says "contact a vet or agro-vet".

Key: 🔴 Emergency / call vet now · 🟠 High · 🟡 Medium · 🧍 Spreads to people (zoonotic) · 📢 Notifiable (must be reported to the vet office within 24 hours)

## 1. Laws and rules the app must respect

| Rule | What it means for FugaBora |
|---|---|
| **Animal Diseases Act** | The Director of Veterinary Services can order quarantine, movement bans, isolation or culling. Some diseases are **notifiable**. |
| **Notifiable diseases** | Owners, private vets and community animal health workers must report suspected cases (e.g. ASF, FMD, anthrax) within **24 hours**. The app should show a "Report to the District Veterinary Officer" message for these. |
| **Animal Diseases (Vaccine and Vaccination) Regulations, 2020** | Livestock must be vaccinated against strategic diseases. District Veterinary Officers (DVOs) set the place, date and time for official campaigns, and owners must bring their animals. |
| **Acaricides Application and Management Regulations** | Compulsory dipping or spraying against ticks. The app's reminders should include dipping. |
| **Standard Treatment Guidelines for Animal Diseases in Tanzania** (Ministry / VCT) | The official treatment reference. Treatment decisions belong to vets. |
| **Veterinary Council of Tanzania (VCT)** | Regulates veterinary practice. FugaBora content must be reviewed before the pilot. |

### How diagnosis is confirmed (for the app's "next steps" screen)
Farmer → District Veterinary Officer (DVO) → samples sent to a **TVLA** lab (11 zonal/regional centres, e.g. Arusha, Mwanza, Dodoma, Tanga, plus VVBD Tanga and Kigoma for tsetse diseases).
TVLA tests: **bacteriology** (anthrax, blackquarter, brucellosis), **protozoology and helminthology** (blood parasites like ECF, worms), **serology and PCR** (viruses like FMD and PPR).

### TVLA locally produced vaccines
| Vaccine | Protects against | Animal |
|---|---|---|
| **I-2 Newcastle** (heat-tolerant) | Newcastle / Kideri / Mdondo | Chickens |
| **CAPRIVAC** | CCPP (Homa ya mapafu ya mbuzi) | Goats |
| **BOVIVAC** (T1/44 strain) | CBPP (Homa ya mapafu ya ng'ombe) | Cattle |
| **Anthrax spore vaccine** | Kimeta (anthrax) | Cattle, sheep, goats |
| **Blackquarter vaccine** | Chambavu | Cattle |

## 2. Ng'ombe (Cattle)

| Disease | Swahili / local name | Main signs | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| East Coast Fever (ECF) | Ndigana kali | Swollen lymph nodes (below ear, in front of shoulder), high fever, frothy nasal discharge, coughing, cloudy eyes. Spread by brown ear tick. | 🔴 |
| Anaplasmosis | Ndigana baridi | Severe anaemia, fever, yellow eye and gum membranes, constipation | 🟠 |
| Babesiosis (Redwater) | Mkojo mwekundu | Fever, anaemia, yellow membranes, dark red or coffee-coloured urine | 🔴 |
| Heartwater * | Maji ya moyo | High fever, circling, head pressing, fits, sudden death | 🔴 |
| Contagious Bovine Pleuropneumonia (CBPP) | Homa ya mapafu ya ng'ombe | High fever, hard breathing with neck stretched out, open mouth, grunting, persistent cough | 🔴 📢 |
| Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD) | Ugonjwa wa miguu na midomo | Fever, drooling, blisters on tongue, gums and between hooves, lameness, milk drop | 🟠 📢 |
| Lumpy Skin Disease (LSD) | Ugonjwa wa ngozi / Mapele | Fever, large painful lumps all over the skin, milk drop. Spread by biting insects. | 🟠 📢 |
| Blackquarter | Chambavu | Hot painful swelling of a leg or shoulder muscle that crackles when pressed, lameness, quick death, mostly young cattle | 🔴 |
| Anthrax | Kimeta | Sudden death; dark blood from nose, mouth and anus; carcass bloats fast and doesn't stiffen | 🔴 🧍 📢 |
| Brucellosis | Ugonjwa wa kutupa mimba | Abortions in late pregnancy, retained afterbirth, infertility, swollen knee joints | 🟠 🧍 |
| Rift Valley Fever (RVF) | Homa ya Bonde la Ufa | Many abortions and young-animal deaths after heavy rains | 🔴 🧍 📢 |
| Trypanosomiasis | Nagana (also called Ndorobo) | Slow weight loss, anaemia, fever that comes and goes, tsetse-fly areas | 🟠 |
| Bovine Viral Diarrhoea (BVD) | — | Abortion and poor fertility, mouth sores, diarrhoea, weak calves | 🟡 |
| Mastitis | Ugonjwa wa kiwele | Swollen hot udder, clots or blood in milk, milk drop. Hidden (subclinical) mastitis is very common. | 🟡 |
| Bloat * | Kuvimbiwa / Tumbo kujaa gesi | Left side of belly swells up, hard breathing, distress | 🔴 |
| Worms * | Minyoo | Weight loss, diarrhoea, rough coat, pale membranes | 🟡 |

| Coccidiosis * | Kuhara damu | Bloody droppings, ruffled feathers, young birds | 🟠 |
| CRD / Coryza * | Mafua ya kuku | Sneezing, nasal discharge, swollen face | 🟡 |

## 8. Team reference only: treatments and withdrawal periods

**NOT for the app. Copy-checked from AI summaries; verify every line against the official Standard Treatment Guidelines with a vet.**

| Disease | Treatment approach (vet only) | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| CBPP | Antibiotics only in early stages; advanced cases isolated or slaughtered | Annual BOVIVAC vaccination |
| CCPP | Antibiotics given by a vet | Annual CAPRIVAC vaccination |
| FMD | No cure; supportive care and wound cleaning | Ring vaccination near borders and wildlife areas; movement control |
| PPR | Supportive care, prevent secondary infection | Mass vaccination; long-lasting immunity |
| ASF | **No treatment** | Fencing, quarantine, culling, safe burial |
| Anthrax | Vet treats exposed animals early. **Never open the carcass** (opening lets it form long-lasting spores). | Annual spore vaccine |
| Brucellosis | Treatment discouraged (animals stay carriers) | Vaccinate heifer calves at 3–8 months (S19 cattle, Rev 1 sheep/goats); boil or pasteurise milk |
| Rabies | **No treatment** once signs show | Compulsory annual vaccination of dogs and cats ("Zero by 30" goal) |
| ECF | Specific drug given by a vet | ITM vaccination plus regular dipping |
| Anaplasmosis / Babesiosis | Specific drugs given by a vet | Tick control |

Withdrawal periods (time to wait before selling milk or meat after treatment) as listed in the pasted summary:

| Drug group | Used for | Milk | Meat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxytetracycline LA 20% | CBPP, CCPP, secondary infections | 7 days | 28 days |
| Penicillin–Streptomycin | Anthrax, bacterial infections | 3 days | 30 days |
| Buparvaquone | ECF | 2 days | 42 days |
| Imidocarb dipropionate | Babesiosis, anaplasmosis | 6 days | 28 days |
| Albendazole / Ivermectin | Worms, external parasites | 3–5 days | 14–28 days |

*Idea for the app: instead of doses, show a general message: "After any treatment, ask the vet how many days to wait before selling milk, eggs or meat."*
